# v0.1 finite-carrier capability matrix

Status: normative planning contract for the v0.1 analysis layer. It prevents a
single finite operation table from acquiring undeclared algebraic structure.

## Vocabulary

- A **bare finite magma** is a finite carrier with one declared total binary
  operation. It has no distinguished element, addition, scalar action,
  involution, or norm unless separately declared.
- A **pointed finite magma** is a bare finite magma plus one or more named
  distinguished carrier elements. A point named `zero` or `one` is metadata
  until the relevant equations have been checked.
- A **finite quasigroup** is a finite magma whose left and right translations
  are bijections. This is a checked property, not a consequence of totality.
- A **finite loop** is a finite quasigroup with a checked two-sided identity.
- A **finite ring** has separately declared addition, multiplication, additive
  zero and inverse, and the chosen ring axioms. In this document, `ring` does
  not imply a multiplicative identity unless one is declared and checked. One operation table does not imply ring addition.
- A **module-backed algebra** is a finite-rank free module over a declared exact
  coefficient domain together with a multilinear operation. Its finite basis
  does not make its scalar carrier finite. It is therefore a finite-basis
  object, not automatically a member of the finite-carrier census.

The v0.1 census enumerates a bounded finite-carrier operation-table domain. The
module bridge analyzes a separately supplied finite-basis algebra. Results may
be compared only through an explicit construction or certificate; matching
dimensions or table shapes do not merge these domains.

## Result-field contract

Every requested property produces one immutable field record with:

- `field_id`: the stable identifier below;
- `status`: exactly `Complete`, `Bounded`, `RejectionOnly`, `Unsupported`, or
  `Failed`;
- `value`: a boolean, exact finite tuple, exact dimension, or `None` according
  to the field schema;
- `algorithm`: a stable allow-listed algorithm identifier;
- `hypotheses`: the exact declared operations, points, laws, scalar domain, and
  theorem reductions used;
- `bounds`: semantic work ceilings, with observational time kept separate;
- `examined_count` and `expected_count`: exact non-negative counts whenever a
  finite exhaustive domain is claimed;
- `witness`: the first canonical counterexample, positive certificate, or
  stopping witness when the status requires one; and
- `unsupported_code`: one allow-listed reason only when status is
  `Unsupported`.

`Complete` means the declared finite domain was exhausted or a recorded exact
reduction covered it. `Bounded` retains checked work and the next unexamined
location. `RejectionOnly` can disprove a proposed match but cannot establish
one. `Unsupported` means the hypotheses are absent. `Failed` means execution
could not produce sound mathematical evidence. Unsupported is not false.
Unsupported is not the number zero. A missing ideal operation therefore never
becomes an empty ideal list or dimension zero.

Allow-listed unsupported codes include:

- `missing_binary_operation`
- `missing_distinguished_element`
- `missing_quasigroup_laws`
- `missing_additive_structure`
- `missing_module_structure`
- `unsupported_scalar_domain`
- `missing_involution`
- `missing_quadratic_form`
- `missing_unit`
- `undefined_power_policy`
- `not_requested`

## Capability matrix by structure

| Structure supplied | Intrinsic finite checks | Checks requiring more declarations | Never inferred |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bare finite magma | binary equations, translations, idempotents, nuclei, commutant, center, closed subsets, congruences | nilpotence needs a zero and power policy | addition, ideals, derivations, norms |
| Pointed finite magma | all bare checks plus identity/zero equations for named points | inverse language needs a verified identity; nilpotence needs a power policy | that a point's name proves its laws |
| Finite quasigroup | magma checks plus bijective translations and division solutions | a loop additionally needs a verified two-sided identity | associativity or group structure |
| Finite loop | loop and Moufang-style equations, inverses as unique division solutions | norm statements need explicit norm data | addition, scalar structure, anisotropy |
| Finite ring | separately declared additive and multiplicative laws, subrings, ring congruences | unital units need a declared checked multiplicative identity | a ring from one multiplication table |
| Module-backed algebra | exact linear nuclei, center, ideals, derivations, units, spans, bounded fingerprint | supported exact field and explicit multilinear structure | finite carrier, division, classification |
| Composition control | explicit conjugation, real part, trace, norm, norm composition, isotropy | inverse formula needs a checked unit and nonzero invertible norm | that split forms are division algebras |

## Positive, negative, and boundary controls

Positive control: the two-element XOR table is a complete finite loop. Its
identity, commutativity, associativity, nucleus, commutant, center, and
congruence reports may be `Complete` when their exact counts match their
declared domains.

Negative control: the octonion table's first canonical associator
counterexample makes associativity false without making the report failed. The
same table can have complete flexibility and alternativity reports.

Unsupported control: asking a bare three-element magma for derivations or
linear ideals returns `Unsupported` with `missing_module_structure`; it does
not return dimension zero or an empty tuple.

Boundary controls:

- An empty carrier with positive-arity total operation has the unique empty
  function, but identity and zero searches return complete empty solution
  tuples. A nullary operation on an empty carrier is unsatisfiable.
- A one-element carrier has one binary table. Its unique element can satisfy
  both identity and zero equations; field names remain distinct even when
  values coincide.
- A zero work ceiling returns `Bounded` before consuming a candidate iterable
  whenever work would be required.
- A pointed element outside the carrier is invalid input, not `Unsupported`.
- A counterexample is recorded in canonical assignment order, independently of
  hash or mapping order.

## Determinism and evidence

Report field order is exactly the order of the table above. Carrier tuples,
assignments, subsets, equivalence relations, and witnesses use their declared
canonical order. Diagnostics are structured and address-free. Semantic records
exclude elapsed time, temporary paths, process IDs, and dictionary insertion
order. Canonical serialization follows the versioned allow-list and canonical
byte rules of the persistence layer.

Completeness claims record hypotheses, bounds, `examined_count`, and
`expected_count`. A stopped search remains `Bounded`; a fingerprint is
`RejectionOnly` whenever it is used to reject isomorphism. Calculation success
does not promote a mathematical result, evidence tier, or scientific claim.

## Non-goals

This document does not add analysis implementation, enumerate rings, introduce
named algebra classes, classify finite magmas, or stabilize composition APIs.
It does not infer addition, scalar action, ideals, derivations, involution,
norm, division, or physics from a multiplication table. Those capabilities
enter only through the later tasks and explicit structures listed above.
